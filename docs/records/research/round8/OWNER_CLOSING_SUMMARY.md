# Round 8 closing note — 15 October 2026

Round 8 has delivered real but narrow improvements. The active allocation is finished; the broader segmentation, simplification and speed goals remain partial. The independent audit is accepted with notes.

Compared with round 7, **six lines improved on three of the 208 development pages**: four more complete rows on 4959 p5; one lower row on 3229 p2 that excludes the following row's letters; and a previously missing body line on 8686 p5. The other 205 pages have identical crop masks. These are not six fully clean, non-overlapping lines: an old neighbour still duplicates one 4959 row, the 3229 outline remains tight around letters and its neighbour still duplicates some ink, and the recovered 8686 row still loses its curled end. The coordinator also previously found one analogous recovered body line on the private pages. This audit changed no runtime and needed no new private run.

We removed six copied ink modules (485 lines), unused caching infrastructure, neighbour-to-neighbour back-print propagation, a duplicate glyph-atlas input route, and repeated image calculations. Those are useful deletions. **The competing size, ink, ownership and row-path decisions remain**, and the live code is larger overall. We have not yet achieved the simpler architecture we set out to build.

The failed approaches now have specific causes. The replacement row model inferred too much from image statistics. The slow detector adapter received tight Paddle boxes where it expected rough regions. Curved-end repairs kept turning local ownership uncertainty into rejection of a whole row. The font adapter withheld detached marks from the measurement that needed them. These failures do not disprove the shared page field or your middle-line construction. They locate the input and consumer decisions still needing replacement. The ink audit also found no confirmed current-leaf loss among the 153 removals reviewed; proposed ways to restore some of them would instead restore ghosts. That is useful negative evidence, not proof that all faint text is handled correctly.

Speed is still unresolved. The final descriptive runs measured about **264 ms median corrections**, with 29–32 of 208 pages taking at least a second and maxima of 2.7–3.3 seconds. Machine load changed substantially mid-run, so these figures prove no round-wide speedup or slowdown. Field proposals consumed about 41% of correction time. Earlier Python rewrites showed roughly 3% overall savings on their paired sample; the tested Rust field arithmetic was exact but 9.3% slower. A language translation alone is not the answer shown by these measurements.

Two decisions remain yours. **(a)** Authorising shared-field crop bands, flat-page/no-curl handling and binding-side detection would let us connect the field to smooth shared boundaries and curled ends. **(b)** Authorising field resolution/pass experiments would let us trade computation against explicitly checked native-text quality to tackle the slow pages. Neither is allocated or guaranteed to succeed.

Your later direction sharpens the first step: **use the nominated edge and stop paying for curvature work across the whole page**. The scout already records a side and window; the expensive field call ignores them. In the saved clocks, all 30 pages averaging at least a second took that route. Line count helps explain its cost, but the recorded count is of final items; an early prediction needs checking against detector counts. No line-count cutoff should decide whether to correct text.

If you say go, I recommend **(a) first, up to four iterations**, beginning with a separately measured restriction to the supported edge and necessary interior context. Require same or better whole pages and every strip, plus reduced computation and paired cost on triggered pages. Then connect the checked field to shared crop boundaries across books, including flat pages and both binding sides. Choose each step after independent review. **(b), up to two iterations if separately authorised**, follows if resolution/pass changes are still needed. Keep FONT parked. These are proposed limits, not an allocation or completion promise. No next prompt or launch; both questions still await your go.
