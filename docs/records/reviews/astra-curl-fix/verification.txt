Curl fix verification (8 October 2026)

Scope and method
---------------
All nine flagged starts follow their own main line in column_segments. _track no longer accepts one point and
uses it to predict the next. It compares smooth endpoint bends against the whole evidence set, anchored to the
existing core. A candidate is a quadratic end correction which joins the core continuously in position and slope.
It is scored with a compact residual kernel, one vote per horizontal letter position; a top estimate is a weaker
alternative to its bottom. A bend needs at least three newly explained positions and two additional votes. The
existing smoothing and line-versus-curve selection then fit the recovered support. Tall letter bottoms can join
an established core, but do not change the initial RANSAC fit or x-height estimate.

Two column fixes are necessary for these starts: close small vertical breaks before detecting a printed rule,
and classify ink separately within each ruled column before finding soft cuts. Otherwise a right-column baseline
can label a curled left word as neighbouring ink and cause a false split inside the left column.

The bounded search tests endpoint slopes from -0.5 to +0.5, with bend lengths 4-24 x-heights, limited to half the
supported width. It does not impose a shared page template. This is an endpoint-curl correction, not a general
replacement for the page-level algorithm or a solution for arbitrary internal waves.
