## Where this sits
The segmentation filter (the owner's three rules: other lines ≤2 whole words; de-skewed long side normalised by height vs Google output through a fitted model, band 0.5–1.5; padding) fails on short crops: the font-line detector needs several letters to set an x-height and roles, so on one-to-three-glyph crops the box is wrong, and the fitted model, built on text lines, expects short numeric crops to be wider than they are. The owner's five-step plan for the filter: (1) whole word = ≥95% visible per word; (2) vertical crops turned 90° right + rotation check; (3) curved lines measured by arc length + perpendicular height; (4) small segments get the box from all ink (no roles, no x-height); (5) the length band re-derived from the owner's reviews. Your analysis feeds step 4 (what "small" is) and step 5 (which population the band is derived for). The segmentation lead runs steps 1–5 through Sol; do not duplicate them.

## The three questions, with the deliverable for each
1. **What dimensions constitute "small".** Distributions of width, height, width/height and characters (px and, where saved, x-heights) over the whole census; where the font-line path breaks as a function of those (0.5 UNCERTAIN "no supported row", DROP "too little/too much", and the owner's "bad box" cases); candidate definitions (by characters, by width/height, by absolute size, by combinations) with the row counts each captures and how many owner-marked good/bad lines fall on each side. End with one recommended definition and the numbers behind it, and the alternatives it was chosen over.
2. **Where small segments come from.** Counts by source, wave, class, and by book/document; concentration (how many books hold 50% / 80% / 95% of them); per-book density (share of a book's lines that are small), with the top books listed by count and by density; whether they cluster in table pages (page-level share). Say plainly whether they are mostly one source, mostly a few books, or spread.
3. **What the non-numeric small ones are.** A typology over a sample stratified by source and by the candidate definition (build on the Opus 120, extend to ~300 looked at by eye with the image tool), counts per type with example line ids and crops, which types are legitimate training lines and which are faults or non-text, and what their Google text looks like (dictionary words, abbreviations, punctuation, junk). Also the mixed ones (digits + letters).
