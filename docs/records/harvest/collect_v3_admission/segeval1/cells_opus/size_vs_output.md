# Crop size vs Google output, short crops vs text lines

Generated by `scripts/analyse.py` (read-only). Definitions:

- chars = non-space NFC codepoints of the Google text (`sum(not c.isspace())`). On the census this equals the filter's saved count (which also drops combining marks/controls) on 592,416 of 592,466 rows.
- ratio = crop width_px / crop height_px (original crop pixels; for the 150 and 888 `after.frame.source_width/source_height`, identical to the census dimensions for all 1,038 rows).
- numeric = stripped text is only digits + `. , : ; / - – — % ( ) + ' ′ § № °` + spaces with at least one digit, or one canonical upper-case Latin Roman numeral (regex `M{0,3}(CM|CD|D?C{0,3})(XC|XL|L?X{0,3})(IX|IV|V?I{0,3})`) with an optional trailing `.` or `)`. mixed = not numeric, ≥1 digit and ≤2 letters (Unicode L*), e.g. `12α`, `Αρ. 7`, also digits with an unlisted symbol such as `12*` (0 letters). non-numeric = everything else (incl. punctuation-only, Greek-iota `ΙΙ`, lower-case Roman). Note `§ 5` is numeric by the given separator list.
- vertical = height > width; excluded from every size table and fit, counted separately.
- short = chars ≤ 9 (rows 1..15 shown so the ≤5 / ≤9 / ≤15 cuts are visible); long = chars ≥ 10.
- quantiles: numpy linear-interpolation percentiles. Fit: ordinary least squares `ratio = a·chars + b` on every row; "median fit" = least squares through the per-chars median ratios (chars values with n ≥ 10). Residual spread = SD (ddof 2), MAD, and p10/p90 of residuals.

Populations: census n=592,466, W1 n=101,098, 888 n=888, 150 n=150. W1 = census rows with wave `W1` (the census reduced from the W1 features snapshot of 2026-10-18 05:24).


## Summary (census numbers unless stated; vertical crops excluded)

1. **Where short crops come from.** Every short crop (chars ≤ 9) comes from the collect-v3 waves W1 (18,815 rows) and W2 (52,436 rows). No collection-v2 wave (round1, round2, mixed, openarchives, cyrillic, parliament, vouli, wikisource) has a row with fewer than 10 characters. In the census, 12.0% of all rows are short; in W1, 18.6%.
2. **Output types among short crops** (chars ≤ 9, non-vertical): numeric 36,307, mixed 1,312, non-numeric 21,490. A further 12,142 short crops are vertical. Numeric short crops are concentrated in `numerics_tables` (34,689) and in the sources elstat (12,122) and gazette_modern (17,505). See A4.
3. **Fits.** For short numeric crops the fit is `ratio = 0.460·chars + 0.787` (n=36,307; residual SD 0.634, MAD 0.236; median fit 0.466·chars + 0.768). For text lines (non-numeric, chars ≥ 10) it is `ratio = 0.385·chars + 1.995` (n=501,467; residual SD 3.113, MAD 1.747; median fit 0.382·chars + 2.207), or 0.371·chars + 2.446 for chars 10–60. **The hypothesis holds: short numeric crops follow a different line.** Their slope is about 1.2× the text-line slope (0.460 vs 0.385), their intercept is about 1.2 lower (0.79 vs 2.00), and their scatter is about 5× tighter (SD 0.63 vs 3.11). On W1 alone: numeric 0.435·chars + 0.841, text lines 0.359·chars + 2.546.
4. **The text-line line overstates the width of short numeric crops at every chars value.** The observed numeric median ratio for chars 1..9 is 1.20, 1.71, 2.18, 2.61, 3.09, 3.61, 4.04, 4.65, 4.80. The text-line OLS predicts 2.38, 2.77, 3.15, 3.54, 3.92, 4.31, 4.69, 5.08, 5.46 for the same chars, i.e. 0.43 to 1.18 wider than the numeric median. A numeric cell of a given width therefore carries more characters than a text line of that width.
5. **Short non-numeric crops do not follow a line.** OLS gives 0.341·chars + 1.783 with R² 0.20, residual SD 1.846 and a long upper tail: for chars 1, p75 is 3.06 and p90 is 5.20. The median fit (0.406·chars + 0.932) is close to the numeric one, so the central short non-numeric crop has numeric-like proportions; the tails are the typology types (see `short_non_numeric_typology.md`). Short mixed crops (0.393·chars + 1.130) sit between the two.
6. **How 0.5 treated short crops (A3).** Numeric: KEEP 83.9%, DROP 5.2% (too little 3.2%, too much 1.7%), UNCERTAIN 10.9% (mostly "no detected row", 10.4%). Non-numeric: KEEP 75.8%, DROP 16.3% (too little 13.5%), UNCERTAIN 7.9%. Long rows: DROP 1.3%, UNCERTAIN 24.8%.
7. **False rejects.** On the 150, of 18 owner-fine rows dropped by 0.8, 11 are short (5 numeric, 6 non-numeric) and 7 are long. Under 0.5 it is 26: 6 short numeric, 7 short non-numeric, 11 long and 2 vertical. On the 888, of 78 owner-KEEP rows, 0.5 drops 23: 1 short numeric, 11 short non-numeric, 10 long and 1 vertical. 0.8 drops 22: 0 short numeric, 9 short non-numeric, 12 long and 1 vertical.
8. **Owner-good short numeric drops do not all sit at the tail of the numeric distribution (A3b).** Their percentiles within census numeric rows with the same chars are: "29)" 9.4, "54.296" 34.0, "670" 63.1, "270" 92.6, "37" 94.1, "3.237.787" 99.9 (wide, letter-spaced digits, 190×20). So 3 of the 6 are ordinary for a numeric cell; the drop comes from the text-line model, not from an unusual box.
9. **Caveat on "vertical" (height > width).** It removes 7,940 short numeric crops. 1,566 of them have 1–2 chars and are mostly just taller than wide: only 108 have height > 2·width, e.g. "1" at 22×24. The 4,904 with 6–9 chars are almost all genuinely upright (4,876 have height > 2·width). I applied the rule as given; single-digit cells are partly lost to it.
10. The ratio here is crop width/height in pixels. The filter measures the text box inside the crop, normalised by its height. The numbers above describe the crops, not the filter's internal box.
