# Expected CER of state-of-the-art OCR, by regime

Survey date: 2026-10-22. Read-only literature/benchmark survey; no OCR experiments were run. Repository context: `7d975ca142d89fd86d2b043553d5468b9710c13b`. Only this deliverable was written.

**Answer:** exceptionally good supplied-line benchmarks can reach well below one percent CER; historical and polytonic Greek performance depends strongly on source adaptation, typography, transcription policy, and segmentation. Our reported results are competitive historical-Greek line-recognition results, but do not establish a shared-benchmark SOTA ranking or a qualified diacritic floor. The evidence below includes strong research results and older baselines, rather than treating every listed engine as a current leader.

## Reading the evidence

CER normally means character edit distance divided by reference length. Preserve the distinction between corpus CER (total edits / total reference characters), an unweighted mean of line/page CERs, and a median. Also preserve character unit, Unicode normalization, whitespace, punctuation, script and diacritic policy. OCR-D explicitly distinguishes these choices and even documents a bounded normalization different from ordinary reference-length CER. [S15, OCR-D, 2022 specification/current documentation](https://ocr-d.de/en/spec/ocrd_eval.html).

In the table, **L** means recognition of supplied line images; **P** means page/pipeline evaluation; **R** means spatially aligned region/word evaluation. “—” means not reported here, not zero. Results converted from reported character accuracy are identified. WER is reproduced only where the source reports it; no fixed CER-to-WER multiplier is assumed. Every measured value is tied to its test set and source. Validation results are explicitly labelled, and results with unresolved reporting details should not set an acceptance threshold.

## Synthesis by regime

### Clean modern Latin print and commercial engines

**Supplied modern English lines can reach sub-percent CER on UW3**; this is not a commercial-service guarantee. [S01, 2020](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/14/2/000451/000451.html).

For Google Document AI, Azure and Textract, I did **not verify a contemporary, shared, clean-modern-Latin CER test covering all engines plus ABBYY**. Consequently a universal “commercial OCR achieves X% CER” is **unverified**. Old Books is historical English, even in its no-added-noise condition; Hegghammer's page-level results should not be relabelled clean modern print. Its ISRI word-error figures ignore some distinctions that standard Levenshtein WER counts. [S02, 2021/2022](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s42001-021-00149-1.pdf).

The DUDE-derived vendor comparison supplies named cloud-engine CERs, but mixes document regimes, uses older outputs, and has a vendor interest. It cannot establish today's clean-print ranking. The original PDF now returns a retrieval error; the table was checked in indexed report text, so reproducibility is weaker than for accessible papers. [S04, 2025](https://planet-ai.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Update-Whitepaper_OCR-Benchmark_PLANET-AI_EN_final.pdf).

ICDAR SmartDoc provides a useful warning: FineReader's baseline, preprocessing pipelines and the corpus-level CCC method differ sharply on the same captures. The winner benefits from repeated views of each document; it is not a normal single-scan error expectation. [S03, 2015](https://marcalr.github.io/pdfs/ICDAR15e.pdf). ICDAR SROIE recognition is another frequently cited benchmark, but TrOCR reports word precision/recall/F1 there, **not CER**; those scores are not converted here. [S13, 2022, SROIE Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.10282v5).

### Historical print

Historical print has no single floor: DTA19 demonstrates excellent matched line recognition. [S01, 2020](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/14/2/000451/000451.html). Roman Type Print supplies a harder comparison across HTR+, PyLaia and Titan. Use **CER Base**, not the paper's more permissive alphanumeric/lowercase variants. [S05, 2025, Tables 3–6](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s42803-025-00100-0.pdf).

OCR-D is a workflow/evaluation ecosystem, not a recognizer with one CER. Its Tesseract/Pogretra comparisons at the HIP workshop show that appropriate Greek training and region composition matter. A generic historical-Latin model is not evidence for mixed-script Greek accuracy. [S08, HIP 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.06817v1). These are named historical benchmarks; no unsupported aggregate “HIP competition SOTA CER” is asserted.

### Greek, especially polytonic and mixed scripts

The literature moves from difficult unseen-font and legacy-engine results to strong matched-source recognition. Simistira's experiments explicitly distinguish unseen fonts from target-font adaptation. [S06, 2015](https://users.iit.demokritos.gr/~bgat/DIA_web/ICDAR2015_simistira.pdf). Robertson and Boschetti's result includes postprocessing and a small evaluated page sample, not a modern neural line-test score. [S07, 2017](https://doi.org/10.3138/mous.14.3-3).

Later Kraken/Pogretra models address Greek and Latin together, but the HIP comparison still has substantial Greek-region errors. Its coordinate alignment reduces reading-order penalties; it is not ordinary page-string CER. [S08, 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.06817v1). Logios supplies a useful recent line-recognition reference, but the conflicting CER entries and unusually low reported WER need implementation-level reconciliation. Its new Kalchas data excludes digits and Latin-script examples, limiting transfer of the claim to our mixed-script setting. Its retrospective comparison also misquotes the older Tesseract CER, so the older baseline above comes directly from Simistira. [S09, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.21474).

The PG study shows strong source adaptation; it measures text recognition and layout separately, so its headline is not proof of complete page transcription accuracy. The paper does not fully specify a reproducible Unicode/diacritic scoring contract. [S10, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09470v1). The newer critical-edition transformer study offers a strong page result, but uses page macro statistics and normalized, markup-stripped text. Header/line-number policy disadvantages conventional baselines; its Kraken training description and table label are not fully aligned. [S12, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02803v3).

Kraken's newer PP-OCR-derived release is **not a Kraken 5 or stock PaddleOCR Greek score**. Its release slice is useful evidence of modern multilingual performance, with incompletely documented Greek coverage. I found no qualified Greek-specific TrOCR or stock PaddleOCR CER that would support a fair direct ranking here. [S11, 2026 release notes, including older Kraken release history](https://kraken.re/main/changelog.html). A current monotonic-Greek-only commercial CER and a shared Greek/Latin/Cyrillic historical test remain **unverified** in this survey.

### Handwriting reference

IAM demonstrates that adapted handwriting recognizers can achieve low CER on supplied English lines; this is a reference point, not a ceiling or target for historical Greek print. [S13, 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.10282v5). Historical handwriting can also score strongly on a related reference distribution, but Republic's authors explicitly warn that correcting HTR+ suggestions can retain its errors and favour that engine. [S05, 2025, §7.2](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s42803-025-00100-0.pdf).

## Where our model sits

**Owner-reported context, 2026; not independently verified in this survey:** our line-level Greek model covers polytonic/monotonic historical print and mixed Greek/Latin/Cyrillic. The internal in-distribution test is **1.24% CER on 42,038 lines**. The new-source benchmark is **2.3% on 2,253 adjudicated lines**, with **diacritics unqualified**, after **168k newly harvested training lines**, versus **4.0% before**; **904 polytonic training-label corrections** were applied. These figures are sourced to the owner's survey brief, not to an external paper or a reproduced run.

On the owner-reported new-source benchmark, the change is **1.7 percentage points**, or **42.5% relative CER reduction** (arithmetic from the owner brief: `(4.0−2.3)/4.0`). It is evidence of improvement on that benchmark if checkpoint, split and scoring are otherwise held fixed; it does not isolate the harvest's causal effect from label corrections or other changes. The internal result is in the same broad performance class as recent adapted Greek systems, and the new-source result is a promising generalization result. However, Logios uses a matched line split, PG is source-adapted, and the critical-edition study reports page-level means/medians under another normalization. Their precise rates and test sets are in the table. [S09, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.21474), [S10, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09470v1), [S12, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02803v3). **Defensible placement: competitive historical-Greek line OCR, with demonstrated improvement on an owner-reported new-source set; SOTA rank, per-script performance and faithful polytonic-diacritic accuracy remain unqualified.**

## Reference-label noise and diacritics: what floor is justified?

There is **no universal measured OCR ground-truth error rate** established by these sources. KB describes its IMPACT reference collection as manually corrected to **99.95% accuracy**—nominally **0.05% residual error**, a derived complement of the provider's quality statement, not an independent blind audit or a floor for Greek. [S14, IMPACT project 2008–2012; undated dataset page](https://lab.kb.nl/dataset/ground-truth-impact-project). Calamari reports that many remaining UW3/DTA19 disagreements are reference inconsistencies or corrupt lines, without establishing a universal annotation-error percentage. [S01, 2020](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/14/2/000451/000451.html). Republic illustrates how OCR-assisted correction can systematically favour the model that generated the draft reference. [S05, 2025](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s42803-025-00100-0.pdf).

**Mathematical interpretation, not an empirical literature estimate:** let `T` be a fully adjudicated image-faithful transcript, `R` the benchmark reference, `H` the hypothesis, and `d` edit distance under one fixed character/normalization policy. The triangle inequality gives `|d(H,R)−d(H,T)| ≤ d(R,T)`. Dividing all terms by the same reference length bounds the scoring discrepancy; changing denominators requires an additional length correction. A perfect image transcriber (`H=T`) scores exactly the reference's disagreement with truth. But reference noise is **not a hard lower bound on measured CER for every model**: a model can reproduce systematic reference mistakes. Nor can training-label noise simply be subtracted from test CER. The count of corrected training labels in the owner brief supplies neither a remaining test-label noise rate nor a corrected true CER.

For polytonic Greek, a missing breathing or accent is an OCR error if it is printed and required by the transcription contract. Simistira directly identifies same-base-letter/different-accent confusions in its Parliament test. [S06, 2015](https://users.iit.demokritos.gr/~bgat/DIA_web/ICDAR2015_simistira.pdf). NFC/grapheme scoring and decomposed-codepoint scoring count such disagreements differently; visually identical Latin/Greek/Cyrillic glyphs also require script-aware references. OCR-D's character definition explicitly uses NFC grapheme clusters. [S15, 2022/current](https://ocr-d.de/en/spec/ocrd_eval.html). Therefore our unqualified diacritics prevent a defensible numerical floor claim. A useful follow-up is a blinded random reference re-audit, distinguishing label errors, ambiguous ink, transcription-policy differences and model errors, plus separate strict-text, base-letter and mark-specific diagnostics. Any accent-stripped score should accompany the strict score, not replace it.

## Line recognition versus page OCR and segmentation

Supplied-line CER conditions on the crop: it usually omits missed lines, duplicated lines, column mixing, reading order and page-region classification, although bad crops can remain. Page-string CER can penalize those errors heavily even when individual characters are recognized well. A spatially aligned evaluation can deliberately remove some ordering penalties; GT4HistComment does this. [S08, 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.06817v1). OCR-D likewise treats text/layout quality separately and explains how column-order errors defeat a simple string comparison. [S15, 2022/current](https://ocr-d.de/en/spec/ocrd_eval.html).

There is **no transferable additive segmentation surcharge**. Recognition and segmentation errors interact. Calamari reports that the worst **0.1% of UW3 lines contribute 20% of remaining error**, with segmentation, rotation and degradation among the causes. [S01, 2020, UW3 voting evaluation](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/14/2/000451/000451.html). TrOCR's printed SROIE test uses ground-truth line boxes and explicitly leaves detection outside its task. [S13, 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.10282v5). To locate our own segmentation contribution, compare the same recognizer on adjudicated crops and automatic page crops, then score complete page output with missing/duplicate lines and reading order retained. Report character-weighted CER, page distribution, coverage and script/diacritic slices together. Our current line-test CERs alone cannot predict that page result.
