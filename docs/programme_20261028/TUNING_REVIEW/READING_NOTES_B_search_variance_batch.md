# Reading notes B — exploration/exploitation, variance, short trials, batch, search checks, selection (subagent report, 28 Oct 2026)

Playbook = refs/tuning_playbook_README.md, cited by README line (L). Paper pages = PDF pages. *Inference* = the reader's own reasoning.

1. **Exploration → exploitation** (L485–515, L1111–1147, L1255–1290). No numeric stopping rule. Exploitation needs "a refined search space that comfortably contains the local region around the best observed trial and has been adequately sampled" and a "final automated tuning study using as large a tuning budget as possible" (Bayesian). Compute-bound default: "Round 1: Shorter runs… Round 2: Very few long runs on good hyperparameter points to get the final model"; 1–3 rounds; "double-checking our conclusions from the shorter runs as needed". Launch on "strong evidence – not just random chance based on a lucky configuration" (L440–480). *Inference:* one unrefined 16-point study → single long run per width, no seeds; "very few long runs" is plural, we ran one (no hedge).
2. **Retrain variance** (L1057–1110): "before adopting a candidate change, consider running the best trial N times to characterize the run-to-run trial variance" (no N; "In other applications… too costly"). Nado: best point × 5 seeds; Choi: 5 reruns, mean/min/max; Bergstra §2.1: weight near-tied trials by P(best). *Inference:* 0.011 pp with n = 1 per arm is a tie.
3. **Short trials** (L1265–1268, L1290–1366): "there is always a risk that our conclusions at this time limit will not apply to… 20%… let alone 100%"; transfer tiers (warmup/init very likely; architecture likely; optimizer HPs "loosely"; LR schedule unlikely); Round 2 (speculation): extend time at high LR; cosine: keep base LR and extend max_train_steps (Chinchilla); pitfall = extra steps at too small an LR. Chinchilla App. B: cosine cycle calibrated to max steps, >25 % overshoot hurts; lower peak LR for larger models (App. D.1). AdamW App. B.1: optimal wd shrinks with more epochs (λ ∝ √(1/T)). Hyperband §4.4.3: for resource-dependent HPs "performance on a smaller resource is not indicative of that on a larger resource". Smith 2018 p4 contrary view. *Inference:* stretching OneCycle also stretched warmup 10×; peak LR and wd were tuned at 10 % length.
4. **Batch** (L245–251, L283–285, L358–374, L2014–2026): throughput is a gate; the criterion for minimum training time is "the largest batch size that still provides a reduction in the number of training steps required"; changing batch "typically requires starting the tuning process all over again" (LR, momentum, regularisation, steps). Shallue p31: "blindly increasing the batch size to the hardware limit will not produce a large speedup for all workloads"; p25: fastest metaparameters "typically on the edge of the feasible region". Zhang: Adam has large critical batch sizes. *Inference:* batch fixed before Stage B → tuned at the right batch; steps-to-result side never checked.
5. **Search checks** (L800–842, L850–912, L996–1050, L1645–1661, L1773–1798): boundary, density, infeasible fraction, axis plots, curves of best trials; Adam FAQ "If 10-25 trials, tune learning rate and β1" (weight decay not mentioned); large IQR at 6 vs 20 trials. Bousquet: scrambled Halton helps most at 11–28 trials; give the lowest Halton base to the most important variable (LR). Bergstra p291: with 16–32 trials considerable variation in the reportedly best model. Papers' budgets: 100 feasible trials (Shallue, Zhang); Nado 50 + 25 refined + 5 seeds; Choi <20 only inside already-refined spaces.
6. **Choi 2019**: search space "may be the single most important factor explaining the rankings"; tune ε jointly as (ε, α0/ε); ε optimum spanned 10 orders of magnitude; "the onus is on empirical studies that fix ε = 10⁻⁸ to defend that choice"; report search spaces.
7. **Selection** (L1508–1527, L1196–1207, L1433–1506, L965–977): fixed steps + retrospective best checkpoint; evaluate at step intervals (preemptions); eval set "large enough that improvements… can be accurately measured"; small val sets cause lucky/unlucky-step variance. Bergstra p302: with a small validation set, model-selection uncertainty can exceed test-measurement uncertainty. Problematic overfitting = validation *error* rising (L925); no source treats loss↑/CER↓. Karpathy's early stopping on validation loss would have stopped too early here. *Inference:* best-of-8 on 437 lines is optimistically biased; best-at-last is expected with OneCycle annealing to ~0.
8. **Budget/preemption**: no source forecasts preemption churn. PB: checkpoint for interruptions (L1514–1518); evaluate at step intervals (L1454). Bergstra p302: trials can be abandoned/restarted without harming the experiment. Budgets are counted in feasible trials (Nado: 67 trials to get 25 non-NaN). Gelbart: never report divergence as a large objective value.
9. **Karpathy** items that apply: don't be a hero; Adam is safe; verify loss @ init; overfit one batch; visualise just before the net; add significant digits to your eval (large eval sets); fix random seed; do not trust LR decay defaults; random over grid; ensembles (~5 models); leave it training; complexify one at a time.
10. **Other**: pick the long-run candidate for robustness, check "learning rates just above lr*" (L1823–1829) and ~500 steps at 2× LR evaluating every step (L1838–1840); fixes before lowering LR; Wortsman: small models show the same instabilities at high LR; per-width tuning is right (L567–614); the control should be equally well tuned (L1023–1028). *Inference:* pre-register a top-k (k ≥ 2) hedge or seed-rerun the top 2–3; break ties toward the stabler point; rescale wd when stretching; budget preemption separately; report axis plots/infeasible fraction/boundary checks.
