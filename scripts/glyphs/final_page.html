Line Readability Check

No OCR reading and no PDF text layer is used anywhere on this page. Everything comes from the pixels of each crop, plus the median letter height of the same document for crops too clipped to measure their own.

How the verdict is madeFour guide lines are fitted to each line. Along x, the tool measures how much of each zone the crop edge removes: the accent / tall-letter zone and the letter body at the top, the tail zone and the letter body at the bottom (counted only where ink reaches the edge). A line is readable when nothing exceeds a limit, partly readable when some x-range does, unreadable when half the width or more does.
Where the limits come from1,760 synthetic lines cut, inked and tilted by known amounts were read by two OCR models. For the strong polytonic reader (CLLG) the error stays near zero until: accent zone cut 0.3, letter body cut at the top 0.05, tail zone cut 0.6, letter body cut at the bottom 0.1, skew 4°. Fading had no measurable effect; broken or merged letters mattered only at extremes, so they are shown but do not change the verdict.
Target clarityClear when the crop holds one column and one full text line. Ambiguous when a column rule or a wide unaligned gap splits it, or when a second line shows its letter bodies; lines showing only edges do not make the target ambiguous.
Straightened linesUnder each crop: the line as the lines API hands it to an OCR model — straightened along its fitted baseline and cropped to its ink. On the synthetic set this brought the weaker reader's error from 40% to 4.5% on tilted lines without changing level ones.
Known limitsLimits are measured on synthetic Didot-like print with two readers. The text-layer boxes are tight, so many real crops lose accents at the top. A neighbour line can be placed half a letter height off when only some of its letters show. Wide capitals are sometimes called merged.
