Page Segments Review

No OCR reading and no PDF text layer is used anywhere on this page. Everything comes from the pixels of each crop, plus the median letter height of the same document for crops too clipped to measure their own.

Four guide linesEvery line found in the crop gets four lines: top of the tall letters (dotted), top of the short letters (dashed), baseline (solid), bottom of the hanging letters (dotted). Neighbour lines, often cut to their accents or letter edges, borrow the main line's shape and are placed by those fragments. On 150 rendered crops: 96% of visible neighbour lines found, typical placement error 0.06 letter heights, 74% within a quarter letter height.
Skew and bendsPositive skew means the line rises to the right. On 100 rendered straight lines the worst high-confidence error is 0.22°. On lines with a known bend at one end, 34% still have some column off by more than a quarter letter height (sharp curls in the last letters).
Letter ink stateYour rule: a letter is one piece in the middle region, and neighbours do not share columns there. Checked against what a reader would see in the scan: "merged" calls are right 85–87% of the time; about 57–62% of visibly broken letters are caught, and 45–49% of "broken or faint" calls are right. Against the physical print, "merged" is weaker, because scanner blur makes separate letters touch.
Accents and neighbours98.7% of separate accents are found, at 95% precision; a quarter of accents touch their letter in heavy ink and become part of it. 97% of visible neighbour-line ink is recognised as such.
Two lines in one cropOn the 27 fixture lines I labelled clearly double or single: 7 of 7 double lines flagged, 0 of 20 single (threshold set on those same lines). On rendered crops 93% caught, 76% of flags right.
Known limitsLetters that faded completely get no warning yet. A neighbour line can be placed about half a letter height off when only some of its letters show (e.g. the second line of c0b02…l00060). Wide capitals such as Φ and Κ are sometimes called "merged". Fits can join two separate pieces of text at different heights.
